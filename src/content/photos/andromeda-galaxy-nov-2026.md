Andromeda again, but this time with three nights on it from the back garden in Glasgow: 20, 21 and 22 November 2026. Each evening I started on M31 while it was high in the east, then swapped to the Crab Nebula later in the night.

| Night | Subs used | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 20 Nov | 147 × 60s | 2h 27m |
| 21 Nov | 228 × 60s | 3h 48m |
| 22 Nov | 112 × 60s | 1h 52m |
| **Total** | **487 × 60s** | **8h 07m** |

None of the three nights was easy. On the first night the mount hit something at about 21:47 (a balance problem), which cost a few minutes. On the second the Moon was about 90% lit and only around 30° from Andromeda. The third night was brighter again and kept clouding over. I threw out 29 trailed or cloudy subs, plus 78 that the capture PC had already rejected for cloud.

The hard part with M31 is the core. It's so much brighter than the outer arms that a normal stretch blows it out to a white blob. I used a GHS stretch that protects the bright end, then an HDR pass on the bulge, so the dust lanes still show right into the middle.

The camera came off between nights, so each night was calibrated with its own flats before everything was registered and stacked together.

## Guiding

PHD2 logs survived for the third night only. This was the roughest stretch of the run: thin cloud kept drifting through, PHD2 lost the guide star for about 30% of the guide frames, and the RMS was 1.65″ against 1.01″ later that night on M1.
