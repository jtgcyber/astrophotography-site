The most time I've ever put into one target: just over 16 hours on the Crab Nebula, over three nights from the back garden in Glasgow (20–22 November 2026). It's the leftover of a star that went supernova in 1054, about 6,500 light-years away in Taurus. It's small at 840 mm, so this is a crop from the middle of the frame.

| Night | Subs used | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 20 Nov | 91 × 60s | 1h 31m |
| 21 Nov | 442 × 60s | 7h 22m |
| 22 Nov | 450 × 60s | 7h 30m |
| **Total** | **983 × 60s** | **16h 23m** |

The first night stopped early. The power went off at about 01:52, and the last five subs were trailed. The next two nights went right through to dawn with a meridian flip around 01:50, apart from some thin cloud on the 21st. I kept every sub the capture PC passed, because dropping the weaker ones made no measurable difference.

## Guiding

PHD2 logs survived for the third night only. This was the best guiding of the run: 1.01″ RMS over eight hours, or a bit under one pixel on the camera. The gap in the middle is the meridian flip.
